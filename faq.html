Questions & Answers

FAQ

Q: So many megabytes for such a small plugin — what's in there, lead?
Nope, pure graphics power! 🚀 These plugins use Rust with the Slint UI toolkit and FemtoVG (OpenGL) rendering — a portable 2D graphics engine that lives inside the plugin. It brings its own cinema instead of relying on your operating system's wobbly folding chairs. The first 10+ MB are reserved for the red carpet, lighting rig, and popcorn machine. Everything beyond that is actual audio magic. 🍿😎
Q: You call it "AI-first" — where's the artificial intelligence?
"AI-first" means the entire architecture and code were designed so I can evolve them together with AI assistants. The AI is my pair-programming buddy — it helped write the Rust, not the reverb. The plugin itself runs on pure, deterministic math. If it sounds bad, you can't blame the AI — that one's on you (or me). 🤖🔨
Q: Why CLAP only? Will there be VST3?
CLAP is the future — open, modern, no legacy cruft. We use Bitwig and Reaper ourselves. If your DAW can't load CLAP: switch to one that can, or send their support team persistently friendly emails until they wake up. 🦖✉️

Good news: LX AudioLabs is now fully open source! Our AURA framework supports CLAP, VST3, and LV2 — you can compile any plugin in all three formats yourself from our GitHub repository. We distribute CLAP as pre-built binaries; VST3 and LV2 are available at build time.
Q: What are AURA and AGAL?
AURA (Audio Unified Rust Architecture) is our own open-source Rust framework for building audio plugins — CLAP-first, with VST3 and LV2 support, and a declarative Slint UI layer. We built it after years of shipping production plugins on other frameworks, keeping what worked and dropping what didn't.

AGAL (Agentic Audiolab) is our AI-assisted orientation and agent tooling — it maps project structure, tracks health, syncs development skills across workspaces, and helps both humans and AI assistants navigate the codebase efficiently.

Together they form the foundation for everything we build — a clean, modern stack designed for AI-paired development from day one.

Q: How do I install CLAP plugins?
Drop the .clap file into your CLAP folder. On Windows that's typically C:\Program Files\Common Files\CLAP. On Linux it's ~/.clap. Restart your DAW and it should pick them up automatically. No installer needed.
Q: Why do the plugins need a GPU? Do they work on older hardware?
OpenGL 3.0+ GPU required. LX plugins use GPU-accelerated rendering (Slint + FemtoVG/OpenGL) for real-time spectrum analyzers, goniometers, and meters. OpenGL 3.0 is nearly universal — any GPU from ~2008 or newer works (NVIDIA GeForce 8 series, Intel HD Graphics, AMD Radeon HD 2000 series, Apple M1+).

What does NOT work:
• Remote Desktop / VM without GPU passthrough: Hardware OpenGL is unavailable. The plugin editor won't open, but the DAW and audio processing continue normally — no crash.
• Extremely old GPUs (pre-2008): May not support OpenGL 3.0. The editor will refuse to open gracefully.
• Old Intel Macs: Not supported. Apple Silicon (M1–M4) required.

No crash risk: If your GPU or drivers don't support OpenGL 3.0, the plugin's editor simply stays closed — your DAW keeps running and audio passes through unaffected.
Q: What is SNAP and how do I use it?
SNAP exports your complete plugin state — spectrum data, band levels, all parameters — as a structured markdown summary. Copy it from the plugin UI and paste it into ChatGPT, Claude, or any LLM. You'll get mix analysis, frequency balance suggestions, and troubleshooting tips — all from an AI that sees exactly what your plugin sees, without ever touching the audio itself.
